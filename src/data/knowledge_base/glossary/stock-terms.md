# Stock Terms

This glossary defines common words used when people talk about company shares. The entries are short and independent, so you can look up one term without reading the rest. Any numbers used are invented examples.

**Share** - A unit of ownership in a company. Owning a share makes a person a part-owner of that business.

**Equity** - Another word for ownership in a company. In investing, "equities" usually means shares.

**Shareholder** - A person or organisation that owns shares in a company. Shareholders may receive dividends and can usually vote on certain company matters.

**Dividend** - A payment that a company may make to its shareholders out of its profits. Companies are not required to pay one.

**Dividend yield** - The yearly dividend per share divided by the current share price. It shows the dividend income as a share of the price.

**Market capitalisation** - The share price multiplied by the total number of shares. It is a common way to describe the size of a listed company.

**Large-cap, mid-cap and small-cap** - Groups of companies sorted by market capitalisation, from the biggest to the smallest. The exact cut-offs are set by market rules and can change.

**Earnings per share (EPS)** - A company's profit after tax divided by its number of shares. It shows how much profit belongs to each share.

**P/E ratio** - The share price divided by earnings per share. An invented example: a price of 100 and earnings of 5 per share give a P/E of 20.

**Book value** - What would be left for shareholders if a company sold all its assets and paid all its debts, as shown in its accounts. It is an accounting figure and not a market price.

**Stock split** - A company divides each existing share into several shares, so the price per share falls while the total value held stays the same. A two-for-one split doubles the number of shares.

**Bonus shares** - Extra shares given to existing shareholders free of cost, in proportion to what they already hold. The company's total value does not change because of the bonus itself.

**Rights issue** - An offer from a company to its existing shareholders to buy new shares, usually at a set price, before the shares are offered to others.

**IPO (initial public offering)** - The first time a company offers its shares to the public so that they can later be traded on an exchange.

**Blue-chip stock** - An informal name for the shares of a large, long-established and well-known company. The term is a description, not an official category and not a guarantee of safety.

## Why it helps to know these terms

Company announcements, news articles and broker apps all use this vocabulary. A clear definition makes it easier to read them. This glossary is educational only and does not recommend any share.
