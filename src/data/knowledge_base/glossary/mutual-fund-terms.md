# Mutual Fund Terms

This glossary explains the words found in mutual fund documents, apps and factsheets. Each entry is separate and written in plain language for learning. Numbers used in examples are invented.

**NAV (net asset value)** - The value of one unit of a fund, found by taking the fund's total assets, subtracting its liabilities and dividing by the number of units. It is usually calculated once a day.

**CAGR (compound annual growth rate)** - The steady yearly rate of growth that would take an investment from its starting value to its ending value over a period. It smooths out the ups and downs along the way.

**XIRR** - The extended internal rate of return, an annualised return figure that works even when money is added or removed on different dates. It is commonly used to describe returns on SIPs. The letters stand for extended internal rate of return.

**AUM (assets under management)** - The total market value of the money a fund or a fund house manages for investors. It changes with market prices and with money flowing in and out.

**Expense ratio** - The yearly cost of running a fund, shown as a share of the fund's assets. It is taken out of the fund's value, so it lowers the return that investors see.

**Exit load** - A fee that some funds charge if units are redeemed within a stated period after purchase. It is deducted from the money paid out.

**SIP (systematic investment plan)** - A way of investing a fixed amount in a fund at regular intervals, such as every month.

**Lump sum** - Investing a whole amount at one time instead of spreading it over several instalments.

**Tracking error** - A measure of how much an index fund or ETF's returns differ from the returns of the index it follows. A smaller tracking error means a closer match.

**Benchmark** - A reference index that a fund's performance is compared against, such as a broad share index. It gives context for judging a fund's results.

**Index fund** - A fund that aims to copy the holdings and returns of a market index rather than choose investments by judgement.

**ETF (exchange-traded fund)** - A fund whose units are listed and traded on a stock exchange during the day, like shares.

**AMC (asset management company)** - The company that sets up and runs mutual funds. It is also called a fund house.

**Direct plan and regular plan** - Two versions of the same fund. A direct plan is bought from the fund house without a distributor, and a regular plan includes a distributor's commission, so their expense ratios differ.

**Factsheet** - A short monthly document from a fund house that lists a fund's holdings, costs, risk level and past performance.

## Why these terms matter

Fund documents are full of abbreviations, and each one points to something that affects what an investor sees, such as cost or return. This glossary is for education only and does not suggest any fund.
