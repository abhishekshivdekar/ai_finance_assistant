# Market and Trading Terms

This glossary explains words you meet when reading about how shares are traded on an exchange. Each term is defined on its own, in plain language, for learning only.

**Exchange** - An organised marketplace where buyers and sellers trade securities such as shares under a common set of rules. The NSE and the BSE are the main exchanges in India.

**Broker** - A licensed firm that places buy and sell orders on an exchange on behalf of its clients. Brokers usually charge a fee for this service.

**Bid** - The highest price that a buyer is currently ready to pay for a share. It is shown on the buying side of the order list.

**Ask** - The lowest price that a seller is currently ready to accept for a share. It is also called the offer.

**Spread** - The gap between the bid and the ask. A narrow spread usually goes with heavily traded shares, and a wide spread with thinly traded ones.

**Liquidity** - How easily and quickly something can be traded without moving its price much. Shares that many people trade every day are usually more liquid.

**Trading volume** - The number of shares that changed hands in a period, such as one day. It shows how active the trading was.

**Market order** - An instruction to trade straight away at the best price available. It is very likely to be carried out, but the exact price is not fixed in advance.

**Limit order** - An instruction to trade only at a chosen price or better. It gives price control, but it may not be carried out if the market never reaches that price.

**Stop-loss order** - An order that stays dormant until a chosen trigger price is touched, and then becomes an active order. It is a way of pre-setting a reaction to a price move.

**Settlement** - The step after a trade is matched, in which the shares move to the buyer and the money moves to the seller. The time it takes is counted in working days after the trade day.

**Circuit breaker** - A rule that pauses trading, or limits how far a price may move, when a share or index changes by a set amount in a day. It is meant to slow down panic or excitement.

**Volatility** - How strongly and how quickly a price moves up and down. High volatility means large swings, and low volatility means calmer movement.

**Correction** - A fall in prices of moderate size after a rise, often described as around a tenth from a recent peak. The exact size is informal.

**Short selling** - Selling a security that the seller does not currently own, after borrowing it, with the plan of buying it back later. It carries special risks and is regulated.

## Why these terms matter

Most trading screens and news reports use these words without explaining them. Knowing them helps you follow what is being described. This glossary is for education only and does not suggest any action.
