# India Account and Tax Terms

This glossary explains words that investors in India often see when opening accounts and reading about taxes. Each entry is independent and written in plain language. It contains no tax rates, limits or deadlines, because these change. For current rules, see incometax.gov.in and ask a qualified professional about personal questions.

**Demat account** - An account that holds shares and other securities in electronic form, in the same way a bank account holds money. The name is short for dematerialised.

**Trading account** - An account with a broker that is used to place orders to trade on an exchange. It works together with a demat account and a bank account.

**Depository** - An institution that keeps securities in electronic form on behalf of investors. India has two, NSDL and CDSL.

**Depository participant (DP)** - The intermediary, such as a bank or broker, through which an investor opens a demat account with a depository.

**KYC (know your customer)** - The identity and address verification that financial institutions must complete before dealing with a customer. It is required to open most investment accounts.

**PAN (Permanent Account Number)** - A ten-character identification number issued by the Income Tax Department. It is widely needed to open financial accounts and to file returns.

**Nominee** - A person named by an account holder to receive the account's assets if the holder dies. Naming one makes the transfer process simpler.

**Capital gain** - The profit made when an asset is sold for more than its cost. If it is sold for less, the result is called a capital loss.

**Short-term capital gain** - A gain on an asset sold after a relatively short holding period. The tax law sets what counts as short for each type of asset.

**Long-term capital gain** - A gain on an asset sold after a longer holding period. The cut-off between short and long differs by asset type and can change.

**Cost of acquisition** - The price paid to obtain an asset, plus some related costs. It is the starting point for working out a capital gain.

**Lock-in period** - A period during which money invested in a particular product cannot be withdrawn or sold. Some tax-linked products carry one.

**TDS (tax deducted at source)** - Tax that is taken off a payment, such as interest or salary, before the money reaches the person, and is paid to the government on their behalf.

**Financial year** - The twelve-month period used for accounts and tax in India, starting in April and ending in March.

**Income tax return (ITR)** - The form in which a taxpayer reports income and tax details to the Income Tax Department for a financial year.

## Why these terms matter

Opening an account or reading about tax brings these words up quickly. Knowing what they mean makes official instructions easier to follow. This glossary is for education only and is not tax advice.
