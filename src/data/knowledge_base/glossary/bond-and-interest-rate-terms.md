# Bond and Interest Rate Terms

This glossary explains words used when people discuss bonds and interest rates. Each entry stands alone and uses plain language. It is for learning, and any numbers in it are invented to show an idea.

**Bond** - A loan made by an investor to a government or company. The borrower promises to pay interest and to return the borrowed amount on a set date.

**Face value** - The amount printed on a bond, which the issuer repays at maturity. It is also called par value, and interest is usually worked out from it.

**Coupon** - The interest a bond pays, usually stated as a rate on its face value. A bond with a face value of 1,000 and a coupon rate of 7 would pay 70 a year, which are invented numbers.

**Maturity** - The date on which a bond ends and the issuer repays the face value. A bond can mature in months or in many years.

**Yield** - The income an investor earns from a bond compared with the price paid for it. It changes when the bond's market price changes.

**Yield to maturity** - The total yearly return an investor would earn if they held a bond until it matures, taking into account its price, its coupons and the repayment at the end.

**Interest rate** - The cost of borrowing money, or the reward for lending it, shown as a rate for a period. It differs for each borrower and each loan.

**Repo rate** - The rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends money to banks for a short time. It is a main tool of RBI monetary policy.

**Credit rating** - An opinion from a rating agency on how likely a borrower is to repay on time. Higher ratings signal a lower chance of default.

**Default** - A failure by a borrower to make a promised payment of interest or principal on time.

**Government security** - A bond issued by the government to borrow money. In India these are often called G-secs, and they are generally regarded as having very low default risk.

**Treasury bill** - A short-term government security that is issued at a discount to face value and repaid at face value on maturity. The difference is the return.

**Debenture** - A type of bond issued by a company that is backed by the company's general reputation and cash flows rather than by specific assets. Some debentures are secured by assets.

**Duration** - A measure of how sensitive a bond's price is to changes in interest rates. A bond with a longer duration usually moves more when rates change.

**Zero-coupon bond** - A bond that pays no regular interest. It is sold below face value and repaid at face value, so the gain is the difference.

## Why these terms matter

Bond prices move in the opposite direction to interest rates, so the vocabulary of rates and yields appears in many financial reports. This glossary is educational and does not suggest any investment.
