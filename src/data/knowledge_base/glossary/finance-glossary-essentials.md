# Finance Glossary Essentials

This glossary explains common money and investing terms in plain language. It is meant for beginners and covers general ideas, not advice about any specific product.

## Assets and returns

- **Asset.** Something of value that a person or company owns, such as shares, property, gold or cash.
- **Asset class.** A group of similar assets, for example equities, bonds, gold and cash.
- **Return.** The gain or loss on an investment over a period, often shown as a percentage.
- **Capital gain.** The profit made when an asset is sold for more than it was bought for.
- **Dividend.** A payment some companies make to shareholders out of their profits.
- **Yield.** The income from an investment, such as interest or dividends, shown as a percentage of its price.
- **CAGR (compound annual growth rate).** The steady yearly growth rate that would take an investment from its starting value to its ending value over a period.

## Risk and safety

- **Risk.** The possibility that an outcome differs from what was expected, including losing money.
- **Volatility.** How strongly an investment's price moves up and down.
- **Diversification.** Spreading money across different investments to reduce the impact of any one of them doing badly.
- **Liquidity.** How quickly an asset can be turned into cash without a big loss in value.
- **Inflation.** A general rise in prices over time, which lowers what money can buy.
- **Default.** When a borrower fails to make promised payments.

## Stocks and markets

- **Equity or share.** A unit of ownership in a company.
- **Market capitalisation.** A company's share price multiplied by its number of shares.
- **Index.** A measure that tracks a group of shares to show how a market is doing.
- **Bull market and bear market.** Informal names for long periods of rising and falling prices.
- **IPO (initial public offering).** The first time a company sells its shares to the public.
- **Bid and ask.** The highest price a buyer will pay and the lowest price a seller will accept.
- **P/E ratio.** The share price divided by the company's earnings per share.

## Bonds and interest

- **Bond.** A loan made to a government or company that pays interest and returns the face value at maturity.
- **Coupon.** The interest rate a bond pays on its face value.
- **Maturity.** The date when a bond's face value is repaid.
- **Credit rating.** An opinion from a rating agency about how likely a borrower is to repay.
- **Compound interest.** Interest earned on both the original amount and on previously earned interest.

## Funds

- **Mutual fund.** A pool of money from many investors, managed by professionals and invested in a basket of securities.
- **ETF (exchange-traded fund).** A fund whose units trade on a stock exchange like shares.
- **NAV (net asset value).** The per-unit value of a fund.
- **Expense ratio.** The yearly fee a fund charges, as a percentage of the amount invested.
- **SIP (systematic investment plan).** A way of investing a fixed amount at regular intervals.
- **Index fund.** A fund that tries to match the performance of an index.

## Planning

- **Time horizon.** The length of time before money is needed.
- **Emergency fund.** Cash set aside for unexpected costs.
- **Asset allocation.** How money is divided between asset classes.
- **Rebalancing.** Adjusting holdings back toward a chosen mix after prices have moved.
- **Corpus.** The total pool of savings built up for a goal such as retirement.

## Why a glossary helps

Financial documents are full of specialised words, and understanding them makes it easier to read official sources such as regulator websites and fund factsheets. When an unfamiliar term appears, investors often look up its definition before drawing any conclusion.

## Key takeaway

Knowing the vocabulary of finance is the first step to understanding how money, markets and investments work. This glossary is for education only and does not suggest any particular investment or action.
