# Trading Hours and Settlement

Stock exchanges do not trade around the clock. They open at set times on set days, and a trade is not finished at the moment two orders match. This article explains the usual daily rhythm of an exchange and the process, called settlement, that completes a trade.

## Trading days and sessions

Indian exchanges trade on weekdays and are closed on weekends and on published market holidays. Each exchange publishes its holiday list for the year on its website. Special sessions are sometimes announced, for example a short session on a festival day.

A trading day is usually divided into stages:

- **Pre-open session.** Orders are collected for a short period before the main session so that the opening price can be worked out from them. In India this has been around 9:00 to 9:15 in the morning. <!-- VERIFY: pre-open session times on the NSE website -->
- **Normal session.** Continuous trading in which orders match as they arrive. In India the normal equity session has run from about 9:15 in the morning to 3:30 in the afternoon, India time. <!-- VERIFY: normal market hours on the NSE website -->
- **Closing.** The last part of the session sets the closing price, which many reports and indices use.

Times, special sessions and rules can change, so the exchange website is the reliable source. Other countries have their own hours. A market in a different time zone may be open while Indian exchanges are closed.

## Why hours matter

News can arrive when the market is shut. Prices cannot change until trading restarts, so the opening price can differ from the previous close. This difference is often called a **gap**. Orders placed after the close usually wait for the next session, depending on the broker's order types.

## What settlement means

When a trade is matched, the buyer owes money and the seller owes shares. **Settlement** is the process that actually moves the shares to the buyer's account and the money to the seller. Clearing organisations stand between the two sides to make sure that both obligations are carried out, which reduces the risk that one party fails to deliver.

## Settlement cycles: T+1

Settlement timing is described as **T+n**, where T is the trade day and n is the number of working days after it. At the time of writing, Indian equity markets use T+1, which means the trade settles one working day after the trade day. Older systems used longer cycles, such as T+2, and other countries or products may still use different cycles. Check the exchange or regulator for the current rule, because settlement cycles have been shortened over the years.

## An invented timeline

Suppose a trade is matched on a Monday at 11:00. Under T+1, the shares appear in the buyer's demat account and the money reaches the seller by the end of the next working day, which is Tuesday. If Tuesday is a market holiday, the settlement falls on Wednesday. The dates here are invented only to show the counting.

## Why you may not see everything immediately

Even before settlement is complete, your broker's app usually shows the trade as done. Funds and shares may have some restrictions until the process finishes, and the details depend on the broker and the type of trade.

## In everyday words

Exchanges open at fixed times on weekdays and close on holidays, so check the exchange website for the current hours. A trade matched during the day is completed later through settlement, which at the time of writing takes one working day in Indian equity markets, known as T+1. Rules can change, so always confirm with the exchange or regulator. This article is educational only.
