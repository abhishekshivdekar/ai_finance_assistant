# Market Orders Versus Limit Orders

When someone wants to trade a share through a broker, they must tell the exchange how. The instruction is called an order. The two most basic types are the market order and the limit order. They differ in what they promise: one aims for speed, the other for price control.

## Market order

A **market order** asks for the trade to happen straight away at whatever price is currently available. If the order is to buy, it is matched with the lowest sell offers in the queue. If it is to sell, it is matched with the highest buy bids.

The main strength of a market order is that it is very likely to be carried out quickly. The main weakness is that the exact price is not known in advance. For actively traded shares the price paid is usually very close to the price on the screen. For thinly traded shares, where few people are offering, the gap between the screen price and the traded price can be larger. This gap is sometimes called **slippage**.

## Limit order

A **limit order** sets a price boundary. A buy limit order says "at this price or lower". A sell limit order says "at this price or higher". The order joins the queue and only executes if the market reaches that level.

The strength of a limit order is price control: the trade will never be done at a worse price than the one stated. The weakness is that it might not be carried out at all, or only in part, if the market never reaches the limit or if there are not enough matching orders.

## Comparing the two

| | Market order | Limit order |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Speed | Price control |
| Price | Not fixed | Fixed limit or better |
| Execution | Very likely | Not guaranteed |
| Risk | Price may differ from the screen | Order may stay unfilled |

## A small invented example

Suppose a share shows a best sell offer of 50.00 rupees and the next offers are 50.10 and 50.30, all invented numbers. A market order to buy a small quantity would take the 50.00 offer. A large quantity might use up the first offers and continue to 50.10 and 50.30, so the average price would be higher than 50.00. A buy limit order at 49.80 would simply wait in the queue. If the price never falls to 49.80, nothing happens.

## Other order types

Brokers also offer variations. A **stop-loss order** becomes active only when a chosen trigger price is touched, and then it turns into a market or limit order. Some orders are valid only for the day, and others stay open until cancelled or until a set date. The names and rules differ between brokers and exchanges, so the broker's help pages and the exchange website are the right places to confirm the details.

## Things worth knowing about orders

- Orders are placed in a queue, usually by price and then by time, so an earlier order at the same price is served first.
- Prices can move very quickly around news or at the start of the trading day, which affects market orders most.
- A partly filled order means only some of the quantity has traded so far.

## In everyday words

A market order says "do it now at the going price", so it usually happens but the price can differ a little. A limit order says "only at my price or better", so the price is controlled but the trade might not happen. Neither is better or worse in general, because they simply serve different aims. This article explains how they work and gives no advice on which to use.
