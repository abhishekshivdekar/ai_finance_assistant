# What Is a Stock?

A stock, also called a share or an equity, is a small piece of ownership in a company. When a company issues shares, it is dividing its ownership into many equal parts. Anyone who holds some of those parts is a shareholder, which means they own a tiny fraction of the business.

## Why companies issue shares

Companies need money to grow: to build factories, hire people or develop new products. One way to raise that money is to sell ownership to the public. In return for the cash, the company gives investors shares. The first time a company offers its shares to the public is called an initial public offering, or IPO. After that, the shares usually trade between investors on a stock exchange.

## How investors may earn from a stock

Investors often look at two sources of return:

- **Price changes.** If the market price of a share rises after someone buys it, the holder has a gain on paper. If the price falls, there is a loss on paper. The gain or loss is only realised when the share is actually sold.
- **Dividends.** Some companies share part of their profit with shareholders as a cash payment called a dividend. Many growing companies keep their profits to reinvest in the business instead, so dividends are never guaranteed.

## What drives a share price

A share price moves with the supply and demand of buyers and sellers. Investors often point to several things that influence demand: the company's earnings and growth, the strength of its industry, interest rates, news, and the general mood of the market. Prices can also move sharply on short-term emotions, which is why they can be volatile even when a business itself has changed little.

## Common ways to describe a company's shares

- **Market capitalisation** is the share price multiplied by the number of shares. A company with a share price of 200 rupees and 10 crore shares would have a market capitalisation of 2,000 crore rupees. This is a made-up example for illustration only.
- **Large-cap, mid-cap and small-cap** are informal labels for bigger and smaller companies. Smaller companies often have the potential for faster growth, and they often come with larger price swings too.
- **Earnings per share (EPS)** is the company's profit divided by its number of shares.
- **Price-to-earnings ratio (P/E)** is the share price divided by earnings per share. Investors often use it as a rough way to compare how richly different companies are priced.

## Rights of a shareholder

Owning common stock typically gives the holder a claim on a share of the company's profits and, in many cases, the right to vote on certain company matters. If a company is wound up, shareholders are paid only after lenders and other creditors, so it is possible to lose the entire amount invested.

## Risks to understand

Stocks are a growth-oriented asset, and they come with real risk. Prices can fall for long periods. A single company can run into trouble, lose customers or even fail. Investors often manage this by holding many different companies instead of just one, an idea known as diversification, covered in a separate article.

## Where stocks are traded

Shares are bought and sold through a broker, who connects the investor to a stock exchange. In India the major exchanges are the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE). In the United States, well-known examples are the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq. Regulators such as SEBI in India and the SEC in the United States set rules intended to keep trading fair and transparent.

## Key takeaway

A stock is a share of ownership in a business. Its value can rise or fall, it may or may not pay dividends, and it carries the risk of loss. Learning how a company earns money, and how its shares are priced, helps investors understand what they hold. This article is educational and does not suggest any particular investment.
