# Indian Savings and Tax-Advantaged Accounts Overview

India offers several savings and investment options, some of which receive special treatment under the Income Tax Act. This article describes in general terms what these options are and how they differ. It deliberately gives no tax rates, limits or deadlines, because these change. For any figure, check the current rules on incometax.gov.in, and consider speaking to a chartered accountant for personal questions.

## Why account types matter

The same amount of money can end up taxed differently depending on where it is held. Governments often encourage long-term saving, such as for retirement, by offering tax benefits on certain products. These benefits usually come with conditions, such as a lock-in period during which money cannot be freely withdrawn.

## Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)

The EPF is a retirement savings scheme for salaried employees. Both the employee and the employer typically contribute a share of the basic salary each month, and the balance earns interest declared by the government. It is meant for the long term, and withdrawals are generally linked to retirement or specific life events. The rules on withdrawal and taxation of interest can change, so the current position should be checked.

## Public Provident Fund (PPF)

The PPF is a government-backed long-term savings scheme open to individuals, including those who are self-employed. Accounts are usually opened through banks or post offices. It has a long lock-in period, and the interest rate is set by the government and reviewed periodically. Investors often describe it as a low-risk, long-horizon option, while noting that returns are linked to the declared rate and not to market performance.

## National Pension System (NPS)

The NPS is a voluntary pension scheme regulated by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority. Contributions are invested in a mix of asset classes, such as equity, corporate bonds and government securities, and the contributor can usually choose how much goes into each within set rules. Because the money is meant for retirement, there are restrictions on early withdrawal, and part of the accumulated amount is generally used to buy an annuity at retirement. Rules on withdrawal and taxation have been revised over time.

## Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS)

An ELSS is a type of mutual fund that invests mostly in shares and comes with a mandatory lock-in period. Since it invests in the stock market, its value can rise or fall, and it carries market risk, unlike government-declared-rate schemes. ELSS is offered by asset management companies and regulated by SEBI, and AMFI publishes industry information about mutual funds.

## Bank fixed deposits and other options

Bank fixed deposits pay a fixed interest rate for a chosen term and are popular for their predictability. Interest is generally taxable as income. Other instruments include recurring deposits, government savings schemes and sovereign gold bonds, each with its own rules.

## Taxes on investment gains in general

When investments such as shares or mutual funds are sold at a profit, the profit is called a capital gain. In India, gains are usually classified as short term or long term depending on how long the asset was held, and the treatment differs by asset type. Dividends and interest are also generally taxable as income. The exact classification periods and rates have been revised several times, so verify the current rules on incometax.gov.in before relying on any number.

## Questions people often ask themselves

- Is my money locked in, and for how long?
- Is the return fixed, or does it depend on markets?
- How are the returns taxed when withdrawn?
- Does this suit the time horizon of my goal?

## Key takeaway

EPF, PPF, NPS and ELSS serve similar long-term purposes but differ in risk, lock-in and rules. Tax rules change, so always confirm current details with official sources. This article is general education, not tax or investment advice.
