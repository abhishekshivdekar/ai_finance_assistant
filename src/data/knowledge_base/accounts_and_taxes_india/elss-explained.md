# Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) Explained

An Equity Linked Savings Scheme, or ELSS, is a type of mutual fund in India that invests mainly in company shares and comes with a mandatory lock-in period. It is often discussed in connection with tax saving, which is why it appears in many beginner guides. This article explains what an ELSS is and how it differs from other tax-linked products. It gives no returns, tax figures, limits or deadlines. For those, check incometax.gov.in and the scheme documents.

## What it is

An ELSS is a diversified equity mutual fund. Investors put money into the scheme, and a fund manager invests the pooled money across many companies. Investors receive units, and the value of each unit, called the net asset value, moves with the prices of the underlying shares. ELSS funds are offered by asset management companies and overseen by SEBI, and AMFI publishes industry information about mutual funds.

## Market risk

Because an ELSS invests mostly in shares, its value can rise and fall, sometimes sharply. The investor can receive less than the amount invested. This is the most important difference from government-declared-rate schemes such as PPF, where the interest rate is announced and not tied to market movements. An ELSS does not promise any return.

## The lock-in

The defining feature of an ELSS is its mandatory lock-in. <!-- VERIFY --> Units cannot be redeemed until the lock-in period ends, and this period is generally described as three years, which is shorter than many other tax-linked products. The lock-in applies to each investment separately. If a person invests through a monthly plan, each instalment has its own lock-in clock. Always confirm the current period in the scheme documents.

This feature has two sides. The lock-in prevents withdrawing in a panic during a market fall, which some people see as helpful. It also means the money is not reachable in an emergency.

## Ways to invest

Investors can put in a lump sum or invest regularly through a systematic investment plan, known as a SIP. Regular investing spreads purchases over time, so units are bought at a range of prices. Whichever route is used, each instalment is locked in separately.

## Costs

Every fund charges an annual cost, called the expense ratio, and it reduces the return slightly each year. Direct plans and regular plans of the same fund can have different costs. Comparing costs is something investors often do before choosing any fund.

## The tax angle

ELSS has been linked to deductions under certain tax provisions, and gains on redemption are taxed under the capital gains rules. Whether any deduction is available to a person depends on the tax regime they have chosen, since more than one regime exists, and on the rules for that year. The incometax.gov.in site has the current position.

## Questions people often ask themselves

- Can I leave this money untouched for the full lock-in period?
- Am I comfortable with the value going up and down?
- Does my chosen tax regime allow any benefit at all?

## Key takeaway

An ELSS is an equity mutual fund with a mandatory lock-in, carrying market risk and no promised return. Its tax treatment depends on current rules. This article is educational and does not recommend any fund.

## In everyday words

"What is ELSS, and is it safe?" It is a mutual fund that puts your money mostly into company shares, with a rule that you cannot take the money out for a few years. Since share prices move, the value can go down as well as up. It is different from PPF, where the government announces the rate.
