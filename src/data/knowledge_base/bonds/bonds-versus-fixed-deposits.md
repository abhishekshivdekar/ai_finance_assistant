# Bonds Versus Fixed Deposits

Both bonds and fixed deposits (FDs) are ways of lending money in exchange for interest, and both are often described as fixed-income choices. They differ in several practical ways, and understanding the differences clears up a common point of confusion. This article is a comparison for learning purposes, not a view on which is better.

## What each one is

- A **fixed deposit** is an agreement between a depositor and a bank or similar institution. The money is placed for a set period at a stated interest rate, and the institution returns it at the end along with the interest.
- A **bond** is a debt security issued by a government or company. Bond holders lend to the issuer, receive coupons, and get the face value back at maturity. Unlike an FD, a bond can usually be traded.

## Key differences

**Trading and price movement.** An FD has a fixed value: the amount deposited plus interest earned. A bond that trades in the market has a price that rises and falls with interest rates and credit news, as explained in "Bond Prices and Interest Rates". Someone who holds a bond to maturity receives the promised payments, but one who exits early might receive more or less than they paid.

**Exiting early.** FDs usually allow early withdrawal, often with a penalty that reduces the interest. Bonds are exited by selling in the market, if a buyer is available. Government securities are generally easy to trade, while many corporate bonds can be hard to trade quickly. The article "Bond Risks" covers this liquidity issue.

**Who stands behind the money.** An FD's safety depends on the bank or institution that holds it. In many countries, a deposit insurance scheme covers bank deposits up to a defined amount, and the rules and amounts are published by the relevant authority, for example the Reserve Bank of India's deposit insurance arrangements. A bond's safety depends on its issuer, and credit ratings give a view on that (see "Credit Ratings"). Bonds generally do not carry deposit insurance.

**Interest rate behaviour.** FD rates are set by the institution and change for new deposits over time, but an existing deposit keeps its original rate. A bond's coupon is also fixed, yet its market price reflects changing rates.

**Minimum amounts and access.** FDs can usually be opened with small amounts through a bank. Bonds may have larger minimum lot sizes, and access runs through brokers, platforms or funds. Retail routes are expanding but vary by issue.

## Yield comparison

Comparing returns requires care. An FD's quoted rate and a bond's yield may be calculated differently, for example with different compounding or payment frequency. For a fair comparison, one should look at the effective annual return, the maturity, and the risk of each. A bond offering a notably higher yield than comparable deposits is often compensating for extra risk, such as lower credit quality or lower liquidity.

## Tax treatment

Interest from both is generally taxable, though the specific treatment can vary by product and over time. This article does not state rates. The official source for current rules in India is incometax.gov.in.

## Key takeaway

Fixed deposits offer simplicity and a stable stated value, while bonds offer tradability and variety but with price changes and credit risk. Each fits different needs and neither is risk-free. This article is educational only.

## In everyday words

"Is a bond just a fancier fixed deposit?" Both are ways of lending money for interest, but they differ in practice. A fixed deposit is simple and has a stable stated value, while a bond can be traded and comes in many varieties, though its price can change and the borrower can fail to pay. Neither is free of risk.
